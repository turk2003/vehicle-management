# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The system serves three internal user groups in a Thai-language vehicle operations workflow:

- Employees who need to book vehicles, view their bookings, and report maintenance issues.
- Approvers who review pending vehicle booking requests and inspect approval history.
- Administrators who manage vehicles, users, permissions, bookings, maintenance, and vehicle history.

Users are completing work tasks inside an organizational system. They need quick recognition, clear status, and reliable controls more than expressive marketing.

## Product Purpose

This product is the PEA Vehicle Management System: an internal tool for managing vehicle booking, approval, fleet administration, maintenance tracking, user access, and reporting.

Success means users can complete routine vehicle workflows with minimal confusion: employees can request and track vehicles, approvers can make confident decisions, and administrators can keep fleet and permission data accurate.

## Brand Personality

Reliable, minimal, and official.

The interface should feel trustworthy and calm, with simple color use, clear Thai labels, and familiar product UI patterns. It should support operational confidence without feeling decorative or playful.

## Anti-references

Avoid flashy SaaS dashboard styling, overly playful consumer UI, heavy dark-mode aesthetics, cluttered admin panels, excessive gradients, decorative motion, and visual choices that compete with the task.

Avoid making role-based actions look like marketing feature cards when users need direct operational navigation.

## Design Principles

1. Make status and permissions obvious.
2. Keep navigation role-aware and task-first.
3. Use restrained visual hierarchy so important actions, errors, and approvals stand out.
4. Preserve trust through consistency: buttons, forms, tables, empty states, and alerts should share one vocabulary.
5. Support Thai-language readability before decorative styling.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for text and interactive controls. Do not rely on color alone for status, approval, warning, or error states. Keep focus states visible for keyboard users. Respect reduced-motion preferences and use motion only to clarify state changes.
